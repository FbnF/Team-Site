2025 Season Recap

By FTC Team 21592 | End of Season Journal

From our first meets through the Illinois Championship, this season was about building consistency, improving match by match, and growing stronger as a team in both competition and outreach.

This season was less about one big breakthrough and more about steady improvement. From our early meets to the Illinois Championship, we focused on building a robot that could perform reliably, refining our match strategy, and becoming more consistent in the way we competed.

Over the course of the season, our scoring became more stable as we improved both our robot systems and our in-match decision making. We spent time tuning, testing, and learning from each event so that we could carry those improvements forward instead of starting over every time.

Autonomous was one of the areas where that progress was most visible. As we continued iterating on code and setup, we improved our ability to start matches in a stronger position and build momentum early. That improvement helped support the rest of each match and made our overall performance more repeatable.

By the time we reached Meet 4, our hard work was starting to show more clearly in competition. We played strong matches, improved our standing, and were selected into alliance play. That event felt like an important marker for the team because it showed how far we had come in both performance and confidence.

At the Illinois Championship, we were proud to compete at a higher level and represent the progress we had made throughout the season. Just reaching that stage was meaningful, but so was the chance to present the work behind our robot, our process, and our team.

Judging and documentation were another major part of our season. Through our portfolio and presentations, we worked to show not only what we built, but how we built it. That included design decisions, iteration, testing, outreach, and the way our team collaborates across programming, mechanical work, CAD, and communication.

We were honored to earn recognition during the season, including the Design Award at the Peoria Western League Tournament, as well as FIRST Leadership Award Finalist and Sustain Award (2nd Place) recognition at the Illinois Championship. Those awards meant a lot because they reflected both the technical side of our work and the way we developed as a team.

Outreach continued to be an important part of what we do. Events like Engineering Day, CAD workshops, and other community activities gave us chances to share robotics with younger students and families. Those experiences matter to us because they remind us that robotics is not only about competition — it is also about inspiring curiosity, confidence, and future interest in STEM.

Looking back, this season gave us a stronger understanding of what it takes to improve: patience, iteration, teamwork, and the willingness to keep refining details even when progress feels slow. Every meet, outreach event, and build session added to that.

More than anything, this season gave us a foundation to build on. We leave it with stronger experience, clearer direction, and a better sense of who we are as a team. We are proud of what we accomplished, and excited for what comes next.
